# Multi-round topic graduation

A topic is a project, not a single experiment. Each round has its own pre-outcome protocol, actual computation on real open data, success gate, negative-result preservation, and reproducible artifacts. The next round is chosen from the prior round's evidence, as a human research program would proceed through failure, refinement, mechanism, broader validation, and replication.

A topic graduates only after its cumulative record includes, where scientifically possible:

1. initial falsifiable experiment;
2. at least one genuine refinement or mechanism round driven by the first result;
3. independent replication or external-data transport test;
4. robustness, confounder, negative-control, and failure-mode rounds;
5. a synthesis that reconciles positive, negative, and contradictory evidence;
6. a professional final report and use/translation assessment grounded in the cumulative evidence.

There is no mechanical minimum number of rounds. The graduation review must justify that the cumulative work matches months of serious human research with appropriate computational equipment. A successful screen is a result, not a graduated topic. An honest failure is evidence that determines the next round, not an endpoint by default.

## Restart rule

Under the user's restart decision, prior completed experiments are Round 0 reference evidence. They remain valid, preserved in Drive, and must not be deleted or repeated identically without a scientific reason. Every active topic restarts its full arc from literature and dataset grounding, maps what Round 0 established and failed to establish, then executes new preregistered rounds through refinement, replication, synthesis, and final writing. A current in-flight experiment under the new model may count as Round 1.

## Depth and open-source computation

Depth is the governing priority in every round. Each round must pursue the strongest scientifically relevant question, not the quickest pass. Use mature open-source scientific tools locally wherever feasible instead of shallow hand-rolled substitutes or API-only analysis. Pin and record exact versions, parameters, model weights/checksums, databases/releases, hardware limits, and commands in an environment ledger. Appropriate examples include HMMER, BLAST/DIAMOND, MMseqs2, Foldseek, RDKit, Biopython, ESM/open protein models, AlphaFold-derived resources, scikit-learn, R/Bioconductor, Scanpy, lifelines, and domain-specific tools. Tool abundance is not a goal by itself: each tool must answer a defined question, include controls, and be cross-checked when practical. Compute ceilings and unreachable dependencies are results to preserve, never gaps to hide.
