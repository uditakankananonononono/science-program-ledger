# Sculpted Project Summary - Predicting Neurodegeneration From Protein Aggregation Propensities

## Status
Completed locked negative plus preserved compute ceiling. No disease-prediction product graduates.

## Useful result
The frozen aggregation-propensity proxy failed its primary predictive gate on real disease/control proteins. The planned ESM secondary analysis could not be completed within the recorded compute environment, and that ceiling is preserved rather than replaced with synthetic embeddings or an unreported shortcut.

## What is new
The experiment tests whether sequence-derived aggregation propensity alone carries disease-class signal under a predeclared benchmark, while separating a failed biological proxy from an unavailable high-compute secondary model.

## Why it matters
Aggregation is mechanistically central to several neurodegenerative diseases, but mechanistic relevance does not guarantee class-level prediction. The negative blocks a common overreach: using a generic aggregation score as a disease detector.

## Working application
The useful application is a hypothesis-screening and limitation report: compare aggregation proxies against sequence-composition baselines, expose calibration and subgroup failures, and flag when a planned representation model is outside the available compute envelope. It is not a diagnostic tool.

## Top-lab reviewer questions
1. Does aggregation propensity predict experimentally measured aggregation within protein families even if it fails disease classification?
2. Are disease-specific regions, post-translational modifications or structural disorder needed instead of whole-protein scores?
3. Can a prospectively resourced open protein model be evaluated without changing the frozen cohort?
4. Which negative controls distinguish disease biology from length/composition artifacts?
