# Sculpted Project Summary - Disease Networks From Negative Evidence

## Status
Five-round cumulative project. Predictive modeling is closed; a bounded descriptive and workflow-priority finding graduates.

## Cumulative discovery
R0 showed that ClinVar benign evidence changes disease-network neighborhoods. R1 established that current summaries cannot reconstruct prospective historical state without leakage. R2 found that ranking enrichment transported across archived snapshots while calibration and controls failed. R3 showed the frozen ensemble did not beat a simple conflict-topology baseline and exposed orientation artifacts from mixed outcome risk sets. R4 separated competing outcome risk sets and found simple burden rankings transported for conflict emergence (2.22x), conflict resolution (2.69x), and review upgrade (2.37x), while complex negative-evidence models added no value over those simple baselines.

## What is new
The project treats benign/conflicting evidence as network information, then follows the finding through historical identifiability, archive reconstruction, baseline competition and outcome-specific risk-set redesign. The final contribution is not a high-performing AI model. It is the discovery that simple evidence-burden topology is the stable signal and mixed risk sets create misleading orientation.

## Why it matters
Clinical-variant databases evolve through submissions, conflict resolution and review upgrades. A workflow that confuses these processes can produce apparent predictive skill without a coherent target. The project supplies outcome-specific, auditable queues while refusing clinical-risk claims.

## Working tool/application
A database-curation priority tool can generate separate queues for:
- clean-edge conflict emergence;
- conflicted-edge resolution;
- non-expert review upgrade.

Each queue uses simple burden/topology baselines, reports transport enrichment and uncertainty, and labels itself as database-activity priority. It must never be interpreted as disease severity, patient risk or variant pathogenicity.

## Top-lab reviewer questions
1. Do the three outcome-specific queues transport prospectively to a future ClinVar release?
2. Which submitter/review-policy covariates explain residual enrichment without encoding institutional privilege?
3. Can queue utility be measured by curator time saved and conflict resolution quality, not only enrichment?
4. How stable are neighborhoods under ontology and condition-name normalization?

## Project value
The useful discovery is methodological and operational: define the right risk set, prefer transparent evidence burden when it wins, and close the predictive model when it adds no value.
