# A stopped prospective test of a frozen validation-hazard detector across clinical, wearable, and molecular domains

## Abstract
We planned a prospective test of a frozen validation-hazard detector across UCI Heart Disease, UCI Human Activity Recognition, and OGB molHIV. Fresh source reconstruction passed all prespecified source minima: 920 heart rows at four class-bearing sites, 10,299 HAR observations from 30 subjects covering six activities, and 41,127 molHIV molecules with 1,443 positives and 19,083 distinct Murcko scaffold strings. Before endpoint modeling, we found that the locked artifact did not specify fixed model hyperparameters, the exact configuration registry, detector-dimension equations, split seeds, or complete control procedures. These omissions determine the primary gates. We therefore stopped without inspecting Round 2 outcome metrics. Under the all-required rule, the study is negative and universal scoring is closed. The contribution is an auditable source-feasibility package, a fail-closed review-support dossier validator, and a concrete executable-protocol requirement for a future independent test.

## Introduction
Naive pooled validation can overstate model transport when observations share hospitals, subjects, or molecular scaffolds. A universal auditor that flags such hazards before deployment decisions would matter, but it must itself be tested prospectively and resist outcome-driven adjustment. We asked whether a frozen detector motivated by an earlier failure pattern could identify material optimism in three new domains.

## Methods
The protocol hash was verified before work. Data were downloaded from official UCI and OGB endpoints, hashed, reconstructed, and audited for sample counts, class counts, independent-unit identifiers, licenses, and molHIV scaffold counts using pinned RDKit 2025.03.6 and OGB 1.3.6. The stop decision occurred before any Round 2 endpoint performance metric was computed.

## Results
All source minima passed. Heart comprised 920 rows and every one of four sites contained both endpoint classes. HAR comprised 10,299 observations, 30 subjects, and all six classes. molHIV comprised 41,127 molecules and 1,443 positives with the published 32,901/4,113/4,113 scaffold split; 19,083 distinct scaffold strings exceeded the 1,000 minimum. Seven SMILES failed RDKit parsing and were preserved as invalid/fail-closed records.

The experimental lock was not executable unchanged. Missing exact hyperparameters, configuration IDs, dimension equations, seeds, and control mechanics prevented a reproducible detector test. Accordingly, no predictions, optimism labels, bootstrap intervals, paired p-values, Holm adjustments, or detector-performance estimates were generated.

## Discussion
The planned novelty was prospective cross-domain transport of a frozen validation-hazard detector. This run does not establish it. Instead, it shows that semantic protocol prose can still leave enough analytical degrees of freedom to bias a prospective test. The negative result is therefore procedural: source feasibility was confirmed, but detector transport remains unknown.

A universal auditor would be valuable if it reliably prioritizes human review without certifying safety. Evidence sufficient for a larger grant would include passage of all locked gates under a complete executable specification, independent reproduction, broader external-domain stability, and measured false reassurance. Current preliminary evidence remains weak because no Round 2 detector result exists.

The next funded experiment should be a three-team, containerized rerun: one team completes and signs the executable lock without outcome access, another executes the fixed pipeline blinded, and a third independently reproduces hashes and gates. Only a passed rerun should motivate a larger untouched multi-domain study.

## Conclusion
R2 is negative under its all-required criterion. Universal scoring is closed. The source audit and non-scoring evidence dossier remain useful; efficacy and product-safety claims are not supported.
