Part 1: The "Virtual Cell" Frontier
Trend context: The Arc Institute's Virtual Cell Challenge (2025) represents a paradigm shift—using AI to simulate cellular responses to genetic perturbations. ISEF/STS winners increasingly tackle cellular decision-making (e.g., "Spatial Transcriptomics and Adaptive Multi-Modal Encoding" at ISEF 2025).
1. A Generative "Virtual Organoid" for Drug Response PredictionTL;DR: Train a generative model on public organoid transcriptomics (e.g., from GEO) to simulate how a patient's tumor organoid responds to different drugs. Validate by predicting known drug responses in held-out datasets.Novelty: Moves beyond cell-line models to patient-derived organoids, a more clinically relevant system.Data: GEO organoid datasets, LINCS L1000, GDSC.

Part 1: Pangenomes, variants & the hidden genome
1. The Genome Has a “Shadow Alphabet”
Research question: Can non-reference sequences in pangenomes explain phenotype-associated regulation that a single reference genome hides?
Novel connection: Human pangenomes × regulatory genomics × phenotype genetics
Public data: Human Pangenome Project; 1K Chinese Pangenome; ENCODE; GTEx; gnomAD/ClinVar
What makes it clever: Focus on how absent-from-reference sequence changes regulatory inference, not just cataloging variants.
Validation: Train on one population/resource and test regulatory enrichment in an independent population/resource.
2. Structural Variants as Missing eQTLs
Research question: How much regulatory signal is lost when association analyses consider SNPs but ignore structural variants and repeats?
Novel connection: Structural variation × eQTLs × gene regulation
Public data: 1KCP; GTEx; ENCODE; gnomAD-SV where available
What makes it clever: Build a pan-variant “regulatory disruption index.”
Validation: Compare SNP-only vs SNP+SV models by held-out expression prediction.
3. The Gene That Exists Only in the Pangenome
Research question: Which genes or regulatory elements absent from GRCh38/CHM13 appear repeatedly in specific tissues or ancestries?
Novel connection: Pangenomics × tissue expression × population diversity
Public data: 1KCP; Human Pangenome; GTEx; ENCODE
What makes it clever: Intersection of population-specific sequence presence with tissue-specific expression.
Validation: Replication across datasets/populations; permutation controls.
4. Repeat Expansion = Hidden Regulatory Dial
Research question: Do tandem-repeat changes act as continuous regulatory dials rather than binary mutations?
Novel connection: Tandem repeats × eQTL × chromatin
Public data: 1KCP; GTEx; ENCODE; disease cohorts in NCBI/GEO
What makes it clever: Model repeat length as a quantitative regulatory variable.
Validation: Cross-tissue and cross-cohort consistency; nonlinear model comparison.
5. The HLA “Grammar” Problem
Research question: Can protein-language representations reveal functional constraints in HLA variation that standard allele labels obscure?
Novel connection: Protein language models × immunogenetics × population diversity
Public data: HLA sequences; UniProt; AlphaFold DB; public immune repertoire datasets
What makes it clever: Use interpretable embedding geometry to separate conserved functional motifs from population structure.
Validation: Benchmark against known HLA functional annotations and unseen alleles.
6. Ancient-Looking DNA in Modern Disease
Research question: Do evolutionarily conserved regulatory motifs remain the most robust predictors of disease-associated regulatory disruption?
Novel connection: Comparative genomics × human disease × regulatory biology
Public data: ENCODE; GTEx; UCSC conservation tracks; ClinVar
What makes it clever: Quantify whether conservation and chromatin context interact to predict variant impact.
Validation: Held-out ClinVar pathogenicity and independent GWAS loci.
7. The “No-Map” Genome
Research question: Can graph-based representations improve disease-variant interpretation specifically in genomic regions that are poorly represented by linear references?
Novel connection: Pangenome graphs × clinical variant interpretation
