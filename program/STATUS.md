## Counting rule

A useful result is an adjudicated scientific or methodological finding that survives its frozen protocol and audit. Informative negatives, non-estimability, transport failures, source-identifiability failures, and compute ceilings count when they establish a durable boundary. Active work, held work, preflight repairs, and feasibility GO rounds do not count until they yield an outcome. The ledger records every inclusion and exclusion so the total can be audited rather than inflated.

## Standing quality target

The target is 100 useful results from the 200-topic program. Each new experiment must state before execution:

1. the genuinely new research direction;
2. the useful discovery it could produce;
3. why the result matters;
4. what a top-lab or high-level science-fair reviewer would ask next;
5. locked success gates, uncertainty, controls, and failure policy;
6. a workable open-data redesign path if the original route is blocked.

Every completed experiment gets a full report and reproducible artifact package. Negative results remain first-class results. Reports must be uploaded to the Science experiments Drive folder. Repository commits are also required when a writable science-program repository is available.
