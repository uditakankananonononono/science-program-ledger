Label honest: Mostly yes. “Not a WIN, not an established NEGATIVE, not a confirmatory NULL” is appropriately conservative. The 0.000 observed gain and bootstrap interval crossing zero support a descriptive no-gain result. However, calling it a “headroom DROP” should be understood as failure of the prespecified gate, not evidence that the candidate is ineffective. The ceiling-gap calculation itself is also contingent on the reported AUROC being valid.

Biggest methodological weakness: The model-selection/validation procedure is not cleanly independent. Both configurations were selected from a grid, yet the reported LOSO DEV AUROC is then used to assess the candidate and construct the paired bootstrap interval. The closure explicitly acknowledges selection uncertainty is ignored. More importantly, the stage-1 permutation failure is substantial: the pipeline initially produced a perfect shuffled pooled AUROC because of an aggregation/intercept artifact. Although a correction was made, the fact that the corrected procedure was introduced after seeing a catastrophic failure makes the final inferential behavior less convincingly locked than a genuinely preregistered pipeline.

Stronger design: Use nested subject-level CV: an outer LOSO loop for the final performance estimate, with all feature/configuration selection, scaling, fusion weighting, and hyperparameter choice performed inside each training fold. Predefine the headroom criterion before running it, then obtain a subject-level paired bootstrap CI only on the outer-fold predictions. Separately run a full permutation test through the identical nested pipeline, including selection, rather than locking only the selected pair. This would address both selection optimism and the demonstrated vulnerability of the scoring pipeline to permutation artifacts.
