1) Drop/disclosures: The DROPPED disposition is justified if the prespecified power gate was genuinely based on the DEV estimate and was applied before any TEST outcome was used. The closure is unusually clear that there is no TEST verdict and that the ECG/annotation read was an integrity audit rather than outcome evaluation. The grid-edge extension is disclosed, but it weakens the claim of a clean preregistered search: Candidate 1 was itself at a grid edge and was not extended, creating asymmetric search flexibility. That should be stated more explicitly as a limitation. Also, “20 independent subjects” should mean independent clusters, not simply records; the described 10-DEV/10-TEST split needs that distinction made explicit.

2) Biggest methodological weakness: The power logic is based on noisy, DEV-only estimated effect size/SD with only 10 DEV subjects, then extrapolates that uncertainty to declare the TEST study unable to resolve a ≥0.03 gain. The candidate-1 MDG calculation is therefore highly unstable, especially with SD 0.064 estimated from ten paired subject outcomes. A bootstrap CI around the observed gain does not establish a reliable universal lower bound on the eventual paired-test variance. Candidate 2's much larger SD (0.169) reinforces that instability.

3) Stronger design: Pre-specify the complete candidate family and derive the power calculation from an external/published variance estimate or conservative fixed variance bound, rather than the observed DEV SD. Then reserve the 10 TEST subjects completely untouched until the locked candidate is selected. If the resulting conservative power calculation says 10 subjects cannot detect the prespecified +0.03 effect, dropping is defensible; otherwise proceed to the locked TEST.
