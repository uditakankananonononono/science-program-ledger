Label honesty: Mostly yes, with one caveat. “SCREEN PASS plus a measured NEGATIVE on DEV, no WIN claim” is appropriately conservative. The DEV result supports saying that the implemented fusion underperformed the selected standalone model. It does not establish that fusion intrinsically cannot beat standalone models, because TEST remains untouched and the achieved power is low. Calling it a “negative fusion hypothesis” is acceptable only if that hypothesis was explicitly defined as this locked fusion procedure vs. the nested-selected standalone procedure.

Biggest methodological weakness: the comparison is not cleanly interpretable because the fusion and standalone pipelines may have different model-selection/optimization burdens. Nested selection protects against ordinary selection optimism, but the closure does not establish that the fusion architecture, preprocessing, hyperparameters, modality weighting, and missing-data handling received an equivalently competitive, fold-contained optimization process. Thus the −0.154 pooled AUC difference could reflect a poorly specified fusion strategy rather than evidence against multimodal information.

Stronger design: predefine a single nested participant-level evaluation framework in which, within every training fold, both (a) the best permitted standalone modality and (b) a prespecified family of fusion models undergo identical inner-CV model/hyperparameter selection. Freeze both procedures before evaluating the outer participant. Report paired outer-fold/per-participant differences, with a prespecified superiority/non-inferiority margin, and reserve the untouched TEST set for one final locked comparison.

Bottom line: the DEV negative is a legitimate result about the tested pipelines, but not yet a strong causal verdict that multimodal fusion is genuinely inferior to the best unimodal information.
