Drop justified; disclosure mostly adequate. Given the corrected gate—gain ≥0.03, positive paired CI, and ≥80% estimated power—the reconciled gain of +0.002125 plainly does not support proceeding. The 6.13% planning-power estimate reinforces the failure. The closure appropriately distinguishes DEV feasibility from confirmation and explicitly discloses that the initial +0.0517 result was based on a weaker baseline and was superseded by the stronger channel-normalized reconciliation. It also appropriately labels the planning power as plug-in/DEV-derived rather than TEST power. One caveat: because the closure is truncated at “not a confirmatory p-va…”, I cannot verify whether the final sentence fully discloses the limitation of the power calculation.

Biggest methodological weakness: the power gate is itself based on a post-observed DEV effect and label-dependent OOF gains, with only 11 subjects per simulated trial. Thus the 6.13% figure is not an independent estimate of prospective power; it is largely a function of the noisy, already-observed effect. The small subject count and three-fold CV further make the paired effect unstable. This does not invalidate the drop, because the observed +0.0021 already fails the effect-size gate, but it limits how much inferential meaning should be attached to “6.13% power.”

Stronger design: preregister the comparison and gate before any DEV outcome, with a genuinely independent development cohort or nested subject-level cross-validation for model/baseline selection, then evaluate the locked pipeline once on untouched TEST subjects. Define the minimum clinically/scientifically meaningful paired gain and its uncertainty a priori, rather than estimating prospective power from the observed DEV gain.
