DROP is justified, but the reason needs tightening. A label-free DROP at the power gate is defensible if the preregistered power gate requires an estimable effect under a subject-level held-out design and only six subject clusters would be available for testing. But “no DEV-only screen” is not itself a fatal flaw: with zero model/split/outcome computation, there is simply no legitimate DEV result to use. The stronger reason is that the proposed confirmatory unit is six paired subjects, making the planned effect threshold extremely unstable.

The MDE arithmetic is broadly sound as a rough paired-t-test approximation, assuming 80% power, two-sided α=.05, df=5, and σd is the SD of independent subject-level paired differences. But calling it a “cluster bootstrap MDE” is misleading: the stated numbers are t-based, not a bootstrap-derived power calculation. With n=6, asymptotic/MDE language should be treated as approximate.

The collinearity argument is too absolute. Label is perfectly confounded with time/block order, so a cross-sectional classifier cannot distinguish fatigue from elapsed-session effects. But a within-subject paired design can still test whether EEG changes from the Normal to Fatigue block. It cannot identify that change specifically as fatigue without an independent manipulation/control for time-on-task. Also, because fatigue-block duration varies and exact elapsed time is unavailable, that attribution problem is substantial—not evidence that the paired contrast contains no information.

Registry wording:
DROP — power/identifiability gate. Six independent subjects yield an underpowered paired confirmatory design; the Normal/Fatigue label is perfectly confounded with session time, so a fatigue-specific effect is not identifiable from these files alone. No DEV-only analysis was performed. MDE calculations are approximate t-based planning values, not observed performance.
