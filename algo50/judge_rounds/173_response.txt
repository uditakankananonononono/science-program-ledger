Disposition/writing: Supported, with one caveat. The closure documents a single locked, held-out DEV evaluation and does not call 0.787 AUC a “WIN” or claim clinical validity. “Completed DEV benchmark” is therefore honest. However, “not a gate failure” is procedural rather than evidentiary: because no baseline, CI, or power gate was preregistered, the result cannot establish that the benchmark is scientifically successful. That distinction should be explicit.

Biggest methodological weakness: The single random subject split is too fragile to support much inference from AUC 0.787. With only 51 held-out participants—and particularly 13 controls—the estimate can move substantially with a different participant allocation. The reported threshold metrics are even less stable. The three recordings per subject are appropriately aggregated before splitting, which avoids the obvious recording-level leakage problem, but does not solve split-specific uncertainty or site/generalization concerns. There is also no comparator baseline, making it impossible to establish whether the model adds meaningful predictive value over a simple reference.

Stronger design: Keep the locked preprocessing/model specification but use repeated or nested participant-level cross-validation with an untouched external test set. All three recordings from each participant must remain together in every fold; standardization must remain training-fold-only. Report ROC-AUC with a participant-level bootstrap CI, balanced accuracy/sensitivity/specificity with uncertainty, and comparison against a preregistered simple baseline. If an independent site/cohort is available, reserve it as the final test. That would distinguish reproducible signal from a favorable single split while preserving the no-retuning principle.
