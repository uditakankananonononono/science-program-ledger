Confound finding: not yet “real.” The DEV result shows that the fixed position/session features outperform the tested EMG feature policies on these seven subjects, but that is an exploratory protocol result, not a demonstrated confound. The feature comparison is vulnerable to subject-specific temporal structure, fatigue-label progression, trial ordering, and the post-boundary exclusion amendment. In particular, “time/trial-only beats EMG” does not establish that EMG contains no information; it establishes only that these fixed EMG representations did worse than a positional proxy in this DEV analysis.

Power stop: defensible as a conservative screening stop, but weak as evidence. The MDE/power calculation is mathematically plausible conditional on the stated paired SD and parametric assumptions, but those inputs come from only seven exploratory DEV clusters. They are not independent preregistered variance estimates. Calling this a power-based impossibility result would be too strong. The absence of a label-free or external variance estimate matters. Also, the stated WIN delta .03 is not itself a demonstrated scientifically meaningful effect threshold.

Biggest weakness: the argument mixes discovery of a potential confound with a power decision based on the same tiny exploratory DEV sample. The temporal baseline may simply be exploiting the known structure of subjective fatigue labels across a session. Without an independently specified null/confound test, permutation/simulation check, or untouched confirmation, “confound found” is too categorical.

Registry wording:
DROP — exploratory DEV screen failed admission criteria. In 7 DEV subjects, position/session-only features outperformed the tested EMG representations, indicating a potentially dominant temporal/trial-order signal. This finding is exploratory and not independently confirmed. Power was insufficient under a parametric DEV-variance plug-in calculation (MDE ≈0.115; ≈11% power), so the unit was stopped before preregistration. TEST was not parsed/scored. No claim of absence of EMG information, clinical validity, causality, or generalization is made.
