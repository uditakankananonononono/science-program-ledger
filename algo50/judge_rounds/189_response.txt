Candidate fairness: FAIL as a representation comparison. The candidate is not fairly isolated against its comparators. The strongest comparator is HistGradientBoosting, while the candidate is constrained to logistic regression. A −0.062 gap therefore conflates representation + classifier capacity + nonlinear interaction handling. The fact that the GBM uses a closely related feature pool makes the comparator useful as a pipeline benchmark, but it does not establish that the candidate representation is inferior. The age-band analysis does not fix this: it shows robustness of the observed gap, not its cause.

DEV-gate stop: conditionally sound, but only as a candidate-pipeline feasibility stop. If the preregistered rule was explicitly “candidate must reach comparator +0.03,” then −0.062 is far outside the required margin, and continuing after seeing it would risk post-hoc rescue. The CI is also extremely far from +0.03. However, the CI is not nested, because it is conditional on DEV fitting/selection, so it should not be presented as a definitive inferential comparison. Most importantly, the stop cannot legitimately be described as proving the representation failed.

Biggest weakness: the design cannot disentangle representation quality from classifier class. That is the central identifiability failure. A fair representation test would have frozen the representation and evaluated it with the same classifier family used for the comparator, or compared matched classifier classes across representations.

Registry wording:
“DROP — candidate pipeline failed the preregistered DEV feasibility gate (AUROC delta −0.062 vs matched GBM; required ≥ +0.03). This is a pipeline-level result only; because classifier classes differed, the study does not identify representation-specific superiority/inferiority. TEST was not evaluated.”
