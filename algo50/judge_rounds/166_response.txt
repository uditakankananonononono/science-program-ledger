Disposition/disclosure: Supported as a DEV-only win, with an important qualification. The protocol appears internally consistent: subject split was locked, TEST subjects were not used for modeling/metrics, and the result is explicitly non-confirmatory. The disclosure of transductive normalization is adequate and importantly says it is non-causal. The pre-split describe() over TEST columns is a minor hygiene concern, but if it truly involved only descriptive inspection and no downstream decisions, it is not test leakage. The permutation control is reassuring against a trivial label-driven artifact, but it does not validate generalization.

Biggest methodological weakness: The 11-subject DEV sample is too small for a credible performance claim, and the 0.709 accuracy may be substantially subject/configuration dependent. The subject-bootstrap CI does not solve this: 11 clustered subjects provide limited information, and the 11/11 positive-subject result is not independent evidence of population-level superiority. More importantly, transductive normalization assumes access to the entire unlabeled batch of a new subject, so the claimed method is specifically batch-transductive, not ordinary cross-subject inference.

Stronger design: Keep the subject-level external TEST set completely sealed, but use nested subject-level cross-validation within DEV to estimate/tune the normalization and classifier without letting a subject's evaluation trials influence its own preprocessing decisions. Then perform exactly one locked TEST evaluation. Ideally repeat the outer subject splits or use multiple independent datasets if available. Report subject-level performance, confusion matrices, and uncertainty rather than relying primarily on pooled trial accuracy.

Verdict: Accept the disposition as “DEV-only feasibility win,” not as evidence of generalizable performance. The main scientific limitation is small clustered DEV size plus the unusually strong transductive assumption.
