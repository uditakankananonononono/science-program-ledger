DROP supported, but only narrowly. The DEV results support dropping this particular controlled→Wild transfer approach at a predeclared-style no-skill/headroom screen, assuming the stated computation is correct: AUROC ≈0.378–0.385 across C values, with the estimate below 0.5 and an apparent sign inversion. The disclosure is mostly adequate: no TEST outcomes/features were used, missing participants and single-class exclusions are reported, and the incomplete archive-MD5 verification is explicitly disclosed. However, calling it a firm “no-skill” finding is too strong because only 6 persons were evaluable, and the bootstrap interval is wide.

Biggest weakness: severe effective sample size. The headline transfer result is based on six evaluable people, not 22. Person-level heterogeneity is substantial (.18–.51), and the ~0.15 bootstrap half-width means the estimate is poorly resolved. The controlled in-setting AUROC of 0.565 also does not establish that the underlying task has useful signal. Thus the evidence supports failure to demonstrate useful transfer, not strong evidence that transfer is intrinsically impossible.

Stronger design: pre-lock the entire analysis and evaluate on a genuinely untouched person-level TEST cohort with adequate numbers of multi-class Wild participants, with the no-skill threshold and minimum headroom specified beforehand. Preserve the DEV set for model selection, then report TEST AUROC with a person-level CI and a prespecified comparison against 0.5 and an explicit clinically/scientifically meaningful headroom threshold. This would distinguish genuine transfer failure from an underpowered DEV screen.
