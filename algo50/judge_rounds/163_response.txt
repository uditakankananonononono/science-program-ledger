(1) Drop/disclosure: Justified, with adequate disclosure. If the preregistered rule was to drop when the observed DEV gain lacked sufficient power/headroom, +0.00348 with estimated power ≈0.065 clearly does not support advancing to a locked/tested result. The closure correctly states NO LOCK/NO TEST and distinguishes the observed-gain calculation from a claim of universal impossibility. The HMM transfer provenance and exclusion/merging decisions are also disclosed. I would want the exact prespecified gate and its calculation recorded in the archive, but nothing here requires pretending a TEST result exists.

(2) Biggest methodological weakness: The DEV-only observed-gain power calculation is intrinsically weak evidence for whether the study is worth testing. With only 16 DEV subjects, estimating a tiny paired method difference (+0.00348) and converting it into power is highly unstable; the resulting ~0.065 power is not itself a reliable statement that the design cannot detect a meaningful effect. More importantly, “ample headroom” depends on the chosen clinically/scientifically meaningful effect scale. The packet appropriately avoids calling this universal, but the gate remains a feasibility criterion rather than evidence against the method.

(3) Stronger design: Prespecify a subject-level paired effect threshold (MDE) and precision criterion independently of the observed gain, then require the DEV confidence interval to exclude/clear that threshold before unlocking TEST. A stronger study would also increase the number of independent subjects rather than treating files/windows as additional effective n. This would make the go/no-go decision depend on a scientifically defined effect and subject-level uncertainty, not on post hoc power calculated from the observed DEV effect.
