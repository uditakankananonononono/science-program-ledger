Disposition: supported, with an important caveat. Dropping at the preregistration/power gate is appropriate if the 0.80 TEST-power threshold was genuinely prespecified. The disclosure is reasonably adequate: it explicitly identifies the post-look baseline additions, grid-edge choices, weak literature positioning, and absence of TEST access. However, the DEV result should not be presented as evidence of efficacy because the baseline was modified after seeing DEV data.

Biggest methodological weakness: post hoc baseline selection/correction on DEV. Adding B5/B6 after the initial DEV look means the reported 1.45-BPM advantage and its paired bootstrap CI are conditional on a data-informed comparator choice. That compromises the apparent strength of the DEV effect even though the study was ultimately dropped. The weak standalone baselines also make the effect size difficult to interpret substantively; a 1.45-BPM gain over a weak comparator is not strong evidence of clinical or scientific value.

Stronger design: Freeze the comparator set, preprocessing, model class, and cost-matching rule before any DEV outcome inspection, then use nested subject-level validation: inner DEV for model/hyperparameter selection and a completely untouched subject-level TEST set for the sole confirmatory estimate. Power should be calculated from the prespecified paired effect and variance assumptions before TEST access, with the required ≥0.80 threshold based on the actual locked analysis—not retroactively adjusted.
