DROP supported? Partly, but the feasibility argument is not sound as stated. The observed 0.00014 L recalibration gain does not support a claim that no method could achieve ≥0.5 L improvement. The “perfect oracle” argument is backwards: if baseline MAE is 0.1056 L, an oracle that perfectly predicts FVC could reduce MAE by at most 0.1056 L, so this particular ≥0.5 L gate is mathematically impossible given that baseline, making the candidate/setup infeasible by definition. But that is a gate-design conclusion, not evidence that “no method could satisfy that gate on this setup” in a broader methodological sense. The parent-set endpoint and absence of preregistration/lock also weaken the claim that this was genuinely precommitted.

Biggest weakness: the endpoint itself is invalid or at least insufficiently justified. The outcome is a nonvalidated max(volume)-min(volume) excursion proxy rather than clinical FVC, with unresolved dataset reconciliation and an excluded workbook. Thus the numerical feasibility result may not correspond to the intended clinical task at all. The tautology concern and post-builder endpoint selection further undermine the purported screen.

Stronger design: first freeze a clinically validated FVC endpoint and an auditable person-level dataset reconciliation; preregister the exact split and feasibility threshold before inspecting results. Then compare the fixed baseline against a genuinely pre-specified recalibration model using nested person-level cross-validation, with the untouched holdout evaluated exactly once. If the baseline error is already below the proposed improvement threshold, reject the gate before modeling rather than retrospectively declaring universal infeasibility.
