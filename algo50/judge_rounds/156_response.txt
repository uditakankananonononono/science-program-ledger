Evidence chain: not tight enough for “DISPROVEN independent-person split integrity.”
It is strong evidence of cross-folder raw-record duplication, but it does not establish that training user172 and testing user290 are two different people. The nine-field profile is not identity evidence, and the same recording date is not identity evidence. The 80 exact waveform matches are the strongest fact, but the packet does not establish whether these are duplicated recordings from two people, the same person represented twice, or some archival/export artifact. Also, only metadata-matched pairs were waveform-tested, so the audit is incomplete.

“DROP the whole dataset at screen” is proportionate for this claimed study design; “exclude the affected pair and proceed” is premature.
The problem is not merely one bad pair: the stated person-independent split has not been demonstrated, and the search did not test whether additional cross-folder waveform duplicates exist under discordant metadata. Removing 172/290 would eliminate the known contamination but would not establish integrity of the remaining 305×305 split. I would DROP from the independent-person screen, while explicitly allowing future re-admission after an exhaustive cross-folder deduplication/identity audit. That is different from declaring the dataset unusable for every possible analysis.

Alternative explanation that breaks the strongest claim:
The publisher may have duplicated or partitioned recordings at the file level, while user172 and user290 are not actually distinct people. Conversely, the identical waveforms could arise from an archival duplication rather than two humans being represented in both cohorts. Either explanation prevents the current evidence from proving a two-person split violation, although neither rescues the claimed independent split without further documentation.
