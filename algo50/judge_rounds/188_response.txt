DROP at the power gate: Justified only as a pre-specified feasibility screen, not because the dataset is proven incapable of yielding a classifier. With no model/split/AUC yet computed, this is a design-level decision. The severe sex imbalance makes the intended SLE-vs-control task especially problematic, but that is not itself a power failure.

MDE arithmetic: The reported MDEs are plausible as approximate planning calculations, but they do not establish the claimed drop unless the paired-AUC formula, effective independent sample size, baseline AUC, and within-person dependence are correctly specified. The data are repeated person-days, so treating the 72/73 persons as ordinary independent observations would be wrong; conversely, person-level TEST counts are appropriate only if the model/evaluation unit is genuinely person-level. More importantly, the r=.0/.3/.6 plug-in is hypothetical, not observed. Calling the result a pure “power drop” therefore risks disguising a confounding/identifiability drop: sex alone already carries substantial signal, and SLE is nearly perfectly sex-confounded.

Female-only/matched rescue: Yes, potentially. Female-only comparison removes the dominant sex shortcut and leaves 71 SLE vs 52 controls, but the supplied female-only MDEs remain large (.261/.218/.165). Matching could improve causal comparability, but it cannot manufacture statistical power or solve age, medication, repeated-day dependence, label quality, and possible cross-ID duplication. A properly powered matched design with a prespecified person-level classifier could have rescued the question; this dataset as currently constituted does not demonstrate that it could.
