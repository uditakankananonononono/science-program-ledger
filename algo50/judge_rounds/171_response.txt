DROP supported; disclosure mostly adequate. The reported DEV gain is tiny (+0.00697) with a CI spanning substantial harm and benefit, and the plug-in power (3.49%) is far below a credible detection threshold. Calling this a pre-lock DEV drop, rather than a test failure or definitive null result, is appropriate. The disclosure is unusually important here: the LVEF fraction/percent mistake was caught before fitting/scoring, and TEST waveforms were not opened. However, the absence of preregistration/lock means “pre-lock” should not imply a genuinely prospective decision rule.

Biggest weakness: the candidate comparison is vulnerable to overinterpretation from a small, noisy DEV sample. Only 30 positives are available, while the candidate adds timing/phase features whose validity is explicitly compromised by the uncorrected 50-ms device delay. Thus the +0.00697 comparison is weak evidence for either superiority or lack thereof. The power calculation being only “plug-in” further does not establish adequate design power.

Stronger design: preregister and lock the complete pipeline before reopening any data, apply the manufacturer’s 50-ms correction, then evaluate the candidate and strongest baseline on multiple independent held-out subjects/cohorts, with the primary metric and effect threshold fixed in advance. Keep TEST completely sealed until the locked analysis is finalized. A nested subject-level resampling procedure on DEV could estimate uncertainty for development, but it should not substitute for an independent test.
