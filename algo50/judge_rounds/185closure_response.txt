Label: mostly honest, but slightly too categorical. “NULL (not decidable at this size)” is defensible for the incremental coherence claim because both LOCAL−B1 and LOCAL−DEMO gain CIs cross zero, and the preregistered ~0.2 AUROC detectable-effect note makes n=50 clearly underpowered for modest gains. But it should not be read as evidence of no incremental effect.

Biggest weakness: severe confounding/transportability risk from the sex imbalance. Sex is strongly imbalanced, and the reported sex-stratified and demographic-residualized results do not establish that the spectral signal is genuinely schizophrenia-specific. They are useful sensitivity analyses, but subgroup sizes and procedures are insufficient to rule out residual site/cohort/acquisition or demographic structure. The single-clinic, n=101 source further limits generalizability.

Plain-language reading: partly overreaching. “Spectral features carry person-level SZ signal” is supported as an observed association in this cohort, especially since B1 beats DEMO numerically and sensitivity analyses remain positive. But “robust within sex and in an age-matched subset” is too strong: the sensitivity analyses are small and have wide uncertainty. Better: “Spectral features showed an association with SZ status beyond the tested demographic baseline in this cohort, with similar directional results in limited sensitivity analyses.” The coherence claim is appropriately cautious.

Stronger design: use a substantially larger, multi-site, sex-balanced external test set, with site held out entirely during development. Predefine the primary comparison (B1 vs LOCAL), lock preprocessing and hyperparameters, and power specifically for the smallest clinically/scientifically meaningful AUROC gain rather than relying on n=50.
